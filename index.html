    My story

    I've always been on the quieter side. I'm not usually the first to speak in a room, but I am usually the one still working on the problem long after everyone else has moved on. When something matters to me, I give it everything I have.

    That's how engineering became mine. Somewhere between debugging code at midnight and finally breaking into a machine I'd been fighting for hours, I realised I didn't just want to pass my courses, I wanted to understand things all the way down. Security taught me to look for what could go wrong. Software taught me to build something that lasts. And AI showed me how much is still left to discover, which is why I dream of one day doing a PhD in it.

    Along the way I've learned that the work is only half of it. The people matter just as much: the teammates who stay late with you, the mentors who push you, and the courage it takes to stand up and say what you think. I'm still learning that last part, and I'm proud of how far I've come.

    Business engineering

    Fall 2026 · the course this portfolio is for

    Most of my degree taught me how to build technology. This course taught me how technology companies actually run, and how an engineer helps sell it. The core idea I took away: customers don't buy a product at the lowest price, they buy a solution and the value it creates for them.

      What it changed for me

      I'm a shy person by nature. This course pushed me out of my comfort zone, and I'm glad it did: presenting, pitching and negotiating in front of the class again and again, I now stand up and speak with far more comfort and ease than when I started. For an engineer who wants to lead teams and explain her work, that may be the most valuable skill I've gained this year.
